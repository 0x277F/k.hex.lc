    My eyes linger sideways -- you ask me what's wrong and I say it's someone-I-hate's birthday. You note that if I were to hate twenty-two more people, I'd probably share a birthday with one of them. Later, I apologize and you tell me there's nothing to apologize for. 

    A long time ago, you found in a thrift store in Berkeley a stack of old film slides -- an old couple hiking a mountain, a man who looked like my grandfather with his widow's peak and uncomfortable dignity eating outdoors. This time, in my mailbox I find a package of photographs and you ask, the question printed by someone in Minneapolis performatively on lined paper opposite their logo and motto (``the most sustainable gifts possible'') and a stock photo of a typewriter, if I've had my fill of this type of gift. You're hoping I'm sustained.
